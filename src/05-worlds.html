Things to find

Each world keeps a few things for anyone who explores. None of them are needed to use the system, all of them work by keyboard where they can be reached, and all of them rest when motion is reduced.

  • Pixel - blocks are worth hitting, headings are worth lingering on, Juni at the bottom has hints (and knows a code), and the cat likes to be petted.
  • Paper Pixel - press a plant and it blooms; a seed head blows away. The cat has a weakness for the box, and only a shake gets it out. Double-click empty space to throw it a treat, stroke it to make it purr, click it and it answers.
  • Sunken - scroll and you go down: every section heading shows its depth, the creatures change with it, and the school parts around the pointer. The gauge in the corner has the sound of the sea: water all the way down, and one voice at each depth with long quiet between - humpbacks in the sunlit water, sperm whales clicking in the deep, a far-off rumble near the floor. It is on until you turn it off, and carries on from page to page.
  • Aurora - scroll and the night passes, from the blue hour through the breakup and a corona to dawn, with tonight's real aurora index setting how active it is. The curtain leans toward the pointer, a click on open sky starts a substorm, and the corner has the sound of the night: wind over snow.
  • Kolam - every page has its own kolam for the day, drawn from rules on a grid of dots as one unbroken line, and a new one comes tomorrow. Stay a while and ants find it: a scout first, then a line of them, eating the flour and carrying crumbs home. The first page keeps a paper flower at its centre. With sound on, the morning plays a veena, a koel now and then, and every press is a mridangam stroke.

How it works

Four layers, each optional past the first. A page that never sets a world is Studio, byte for byte.

The contract

Components read a small set of personality tokens - heading face, label case, line weight, lift, press depth, action colour, texture, cursor. Their defaults are Studio, so the contract changes nothing until a world fills it in.

The world file

tokens/worlds/<id>.json overrides any token in three parts: base for both themes, then light and dark. The build refuses unknown tokens, light values without a dark partner, and reports text below 4.5:1.

Flourishes

css/worlds/<id>.css holds what tokens cannot say - stepped corners, outlined accents, dotted rules, the ambient sky - scoped to [data-world].

The switch

js/worlds.js sets the attribute, loads only the fonts of a world being visited, reveals it from the click, and runs its ambient layer. Reduced motion gets an instant switch and a still sky.

page.html
<!-- a page in a world: one attribute, one stylesheet, one script -->
<html data-world="aurora">
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://system.heyrav.in/heyrav.css">
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://system.heyrav.in/worlds.css">
<script src="https://system.heyrav.in/js/worlds-data.js" defer></script>
<script src="https://system.heyrav.in/js/worlds.js" defer></script>

<!-- any button becomes a world switch -->
<button data-world-set="pixel">Pixel</button>

The world contract

The personality tokens. Everything else a world sets - colours, fonts, radii, motion - is an ordinary token from the foundations.

Make a world

Start from a feeling, not a palette

Name what the world is - a platform, a polar night - and decide its type, shape, depth and motion before its colours.

Write the file

Copy a world JSON, give it an order, a name, a one-line line, three chips for the picker and its Google Fonts query. Fill base, then light and dark.

Add flourishes last

Only for what no token covers. Scope every rule to the world.

Build and walk it

node build.mjs, fix every contrast line it prints, then walk every page in both themes.
